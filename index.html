T+0.00s The tap

There is a gap between holding and spending, and everybody here has learned to live with it.

Your money is onchain. It sits there, it does its job, and the moment you want to buy something with it you are thrown back into 2017: move it to an exchange, sell into fiat, wait for a bank rail that keeps office hours, and arrive at the checkout two days later having exited a position you never wanted to exit.

So people do the obvious thing. They keep a separate pot of ordinary money for ordinary life, and the onchain money never touches anything real. TAP closes that gap with the most boring object in finance. A card.

You tap it. Everything below happens underneath, in about two seconds, and the merchant sees a normal card payment — because that is what it is.

T+0.35s The authorisation

Two seconds is not a design target. It is the whole constraint.

A terminal asks the card's issuer for an authorisation and gives up if the answer takes much longer than two seconds. Selling an asset and settling it does not happen in two seconds. Those two facts do not fit together, and every product that claims to "pay merchants directly from your crypto" is quietly resolving them somehow without saying how.

Per-transaction ceiling $2,000

Type a purchase in the terminal above and this fills. Over the ceiling, the authorisation is refused here — before any capital is committed — rather than halfway through.

T+0.40s The float

Our own capital fronts your purchase, and that has consequences worth stating.

TAP resolves the two-second problem with a float: we authorise from our own capital, and your sale repays it seconds later. That is the only mechanism that makes a card work. Everything else on this page is downstream of it.

What the float meansWhat follows from it
TAP briefly carries your purchaseFor a few seconds we are exposed to the price of the asset you are about to sell. Inside the published band we absorb the move. Outside it, the authorisation reverses and you are told why at the till, not later.
The float has a sizeA float is not infinite, and a product that pretends otherwise is lying about its own balance sheet. The ceiling is published and enforced before capital is committed, not discovered by you at a checkout.
TAP takes a real risk on every tapThat is what the fee is for. Not for "access", not for "the agent" — for carrying two seconds of price exposure so you do not have to pre-convert.
The band± 0.75%

If the asset moves against us by less than this between authorisation and settlement, TAP eats it. More than this and the transaction reverses. The number is here rather than in a support article because it is the one number that decides whether a tap you just made is going to hold.

T+1.20s The sale

Only the slice this purchase needs.

Using the priority order you set, TAP sells the amount of one asset needed to cover this purchase plus the published fee. Not a round number, not a buffer, not a top-up of a spending balance you then have to manage.

Everything you did not spend stays exactly where it was — still invested, still yours, still exposed to whatever you bought it for. That is the entire reason to hold a card like this instead of moving money to a spending account once a month.

The units sold in the terminal above are computed from a live spot price, read from a public venue when this page loaded. The venue and the second it was read are printed on the receipt, because a number without a source is decoration.

T+1.90s The settlement

The proceeds close the position the float opened.

The sale settles back into TAP's float, closing the exposure taken at T+0.40s. From your side nothing has happened except that a card worked. From ours, a position was opened and closed inside two seconds.

The merchant is paid on the card rails, on the card rails' own schedule, exactly as they would be by any other card. They never learn that anything was sold, and they never have to do anything differently — which is the only reason this can work at a shop that has no interest in crypto whatsoever.

T+2.00s The receipt

Which asset, how much of it, at what price, and what we charged.

Per transaction, every time. This is the part of the product that is easy to leave out and impossible to add back once people have stopped expecting it.

Run a tap in the terminal at the top of this page and the receipt it prints is the real format — the same lines, the same order, with a dash wherever a figure does not exist yet rather than a plausible-looking stand-in.

One tap, one sale, one receipt — and the receipt says what it cost you.

What you pay

Three things, itemised on every receipt.

ChargeWho sets it
TAP's fee 0.90%Us. Published on the card's settings page before you ever tap it, and on the receipt afterwards so you can check we charged what we said.
The spreadThe market, at the moment of sale. Not ours, not marked up, and not knowable in advance — which is why the simulator prints a dash there instead of a number.
FXThe card network, where the merchant charges in a currency other than the one settled.

No monthly fee. No minimum balance. No tier that unlocks a better rate for people who already have more.

When it goes wrong

The failure cases, in the same size type as the happy path.

Every product in this category publishes the two seconds where everything works. You find out what something actually is from the other list.

What happensWhat TAP does
The price moves between authorisation and settlementInside ±0.75%, TAP absorbs it. Outside it, the transaction reverses and you are told at the till.
Your balance is not enough by the time the sale runsThe authorisation is declined before the float is committed, not after. A decline is annoying; a half-settled purchase is much worse.
The merchant refunds youThis is the one nobody mentions. A refund arrives as stablecoin, not as the asset you sold. Sell tokenised NVDA on Tuesday, get refunded on Friday, and you get dollars back — not your NVDA. If it moved in between, that difference is real and it is yours. We will not pretend a refund is a time machine.
The network is down at the terminalThe tap fails. Nothing is sold. This is correct behaviour and the one case where doing nothing is the feature.
TAP's float is unavailableThe card declines, across the board, and the status page says so. It does not silently fall back to something worse.
You tap for more than the ceilingRefused at T+0.35s, with the ceiling and your amount both shown. Try it in the terminal above.

About identity

There is an identity step, it is the issuer's, and we would rather say so now.

A card has an issuer. An issuer has regulators, and those regulators have rules about who can be given a payment instrument. That is true of every card that has ever worked at a real checkout, including the ones marketed as requiring nothing.

So TAP will not tell you there is no identity step. There is one, and we would rather you knew before you moved money than after.

What we can tell you is what we do with it: TAP does not sell your transaction history, does not build a profile on your spending to market back to you, and does not share what you bought with anyone who is not legally owed it.

Not built yet

No card has been issued, no float has been deployed, and no purchase has settled.

Why a card

The obvious version of this is a chat box. It demos beautifully.

Tell an agent what you want, it finds it and buys it. It also asks you to learn a new way to buy things, to trust a model with your shopping, and to accept that the thing it found is the thing you wanted. Every one of those is a reason not to use it on a Tuesday afternoon.

A card asks you to learn nothing. You already know how to tap. You already trust the checkout. The only thing that changes is where the money came from, and that is the only thing that should be changing.

They describe what an agent will do. We publish what it spent.

Questions

Starting with the one that is least comfortable.

So TAP is lending me money for two seconds?

Functionally, for those two seconds, TAP's capital is what the merchant is paid from — and your sale repays it immediately afterwards. We say "float" rather than anything grander because that is what it is. It also means TAP carries real risk on every tap, which is the honest answer to "why is there a fee at all".

What if the price moves in those two seconds?

Inside ±0.75% we absorb it. Outside it the authorisation reverses and you are told at the till rather than finding out from a statement. The band is published because it is the number that decides whether a tap you just made is going to hold.

Why is there a per-transaction ceiling?

Because the float has a size and pretending otherwise would be lying about our own balance sheet. The ceiling is enforced at T+0.35s, before any capital is committed. Put a number over it into the terminal at the top of this page and you will see exactly the refusal a real card would give you.

Which assets can fund a tap?

The simulator prices BTC, ETH and SOL because those are the three a public venue will quote to this site right now. The product is meant to cover tokenised equities too, and the refund case in When it goes wrong is written for them — but a price we cannot read is a price we will not print.

Does the merchant know?

No, and that is the point. They see a card payment on the same rails as every other card payment, settled on the same schedule, with nothing to integrate and nothing to opt into. A shop with no interest in crypto is exactly the shop this has to work at.

Is there really an identity step?

Yes. Every card that works at a real checkout has one, including the ones marketed as requiring nothing. Ours is the issuer's. Section About identity says what we do and do not do with it.
