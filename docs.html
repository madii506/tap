01  —  The order

The settlement order, clause by clause.

01.1T+0.000 — the terminal asks The merchant's terminal requests an authorisation from the card issuer for the purchase amount in the merchant's currency. Nothing about TAP is visible to the merchant at any point in this list.
01.2T+0.350 — the ceiling is checked Before any capital moves: the purchase is compared against the per-transaction ceiling and against your balance in the asset first in your priority order. A failure here is a decline at the till and nothing else happens. This step exists so that a failure is boring.
01.3T+0.400 — the float fronts it TAP's own capital covers the purchase plus the fee, and the authorisation is returned to the terminal. From this instant TAP holds price exposure to your asset that it did not choose and does not want.
01.4T+1.200 — the sale A market sell for exactly the amount needed — purchase plus fee — of one asset. Not a round number, not a buffer, not a top-up of a spending balance.
01.5T+1.900 — settlement Proceeds return to the float, closing the position opened at 01.3. The band in section 04 decides what happens if the price moved while this was in flight.
01.6T+2.000 — the receipt Written with the asset, the units, the execution price, the spread, the fee and the amount the merchant was paid. Written whether or not anyone reads it.
01.7Later — the merchant is paid On the card networks' own schedule, exactly as for any other card. TAP does not sit between the issuer and the merchant and cannot speed this up or slow it down.

02  —  Custody

Who is holding the money at each step.

This is the question a serious reader asks first and the one most products in this category never answer. Read the middle column as "if everything stopped right now, whose balance sheet is this on".

Note 2TAP never holds your assets as a custodian between purchases. The only window in which our balance sheet and your position are entangled is the 1.5 seconds between 01.3 and 01.5, and that entanglement runs in your favour: we are the ones exposed.

03  —  When it fails midway

The order fails after the asset is already sold.

This is the honest heart of the product and the case every competitor's page stops short of. The asset is gone, the merchant has not been paid, and somebody has to be worse off.

03.1The authorisation is reversed after the sale settles You are left holding stablecoin where an asset used to be. TAP returns the full proceeds to you and absorbs the fee — you pay nothing for a transaction that did not complete. What TAP cannot do is put your position back, and we will not claim to. That is a real cost and it lands on you.
03.2The sale fails but the authorisation stands TAP's float has already paid. We carry it and retry against the next asset in your priority order. If every asset fails, the float carries the purchase and we come to you for it — and the receipt says exactly that rather than describing it as a "pending settlement".
03.3The venue is down at 01.4 The authorisation from 01.3 is reversed before any sale is attempted. You see a decline. This is the good failure: it costs a checkout, not a position.
03.4TAP is down The card declines across the board and the status page says so. It does not silently fall back to a slower path, a different asset, or a balance you did not agree to spend.
The ruleWhere a failure has to cost somebody, the order is: TAP's fee first, then TAP's float, then you — and never you silently.

04  —  The band

± 0.75%, worked through.

Between 01.3 and 01.5 the price can move. The band is the amount of that move TAP absorbs rather than passing to you or reversing the transaction over.

purchase        $200.00
fee   0.90%     $  1.80
to raise        $201.80

inside the band
price at 01.3   $100.00   ->  2.018000 units committed
price at 01.5   $ 99.40   ->  move -0.60%, inside 0.75%
outcome         TAP absorbs $1.21. You are charged $201.80. Nothing else changes.

outside the band
price at 01.5   $ 98.50   ->  move -1.50%, outside 0.75%
outcome         authorisation reversed at the till, nothing sold, no fee taken

The band is symmetric. When the price moves in our favour by less than 0.75% TAP keeps the difference — which is the only reason a band this tight is affordable to offer at all, and saying so is cheaper than being caught not saying it.

05  —  Refunds

A refund is not a time machine.

A merchant refund arrives on the card rails, days later, as money. It cannot arrive as the asset you sold, because nobody in the chain is holding that asset any more — you sold it, at a price, on a date.

So a refund credits stablecoin. If you sold tokenised NVDA on Tuesday and the merchant refunds you on Friday, you get dollars back, not your NVDA, and if NVDA moved in between then that difference is real and it is yours.

Note 3TAP will not offer to re-buy the asset for you at the refund. That would be TAP taking a position on your behalf with your money on a timescale you did not choose, which is a different product and a worse one.

07  —  Limits

What this site cannot show you today.

  • That any of section 01 happens. No card is issued and no float is deployed. Sections 01 to 05 are a specification, and until an issuer is named and a float address is published they are enforced by nothing but this page.
  • An execution price. The simulator prices your sale off a public spot quote. A real sale executes against a book, at a price that includes a spread nobody can know in advance, which is why the spread line on the receipt is a dash.
  • The float's size. A dash in section 00. When there is a float there will be an address, and you will be able to read its balance without asking us.
  • Tokenised equities. The refund case in section 05 is written for them and they are the reason this product is interesting, but no venue quotes them to this site, so the simulator does not offer them. A price we cannot read is a price we will not print.
  • Anything about the issuer. Not named, because nothing is signed. When one is, it goes here with the licence it holds.
Note 4The token does not exist yet either. When it does, the address appears on this site and in the pinned post at the same moment, and anyone posting one before that is scamming you.

08  —  Vocabulary

Six words used precisely on this site.

WordMeans, here
FloatTAP's own capital, used to front an authorisation and repaid by your sale seconds later. Not a deposit, not your balance, not a pool anyone else funds.
BandThe price move between 01.3 and 01.5 that TAP absorbs instead of reversing over.
CeilingThe largest single purchase the float will front, checked before any capital moves.
SliceExactly the units of one asset needed for one purchase plus its fee. Never rounded up.
ReversalThe authorisation is undone and nothing is sold. Costs a checkout, not a position.
ReceiptThe per-transaction record of asset, units, price, spread, fee and amount paid. If TAP stops publishing it, the rest of this document is worthless.
